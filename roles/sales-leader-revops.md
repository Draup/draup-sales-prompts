# Prompts for Sales Leader & RevOps

Size markets, tier accounts, carve territories and focus the team.

90 prompts, grouped by sales job.

**First:** [connect Draup for Sales](../guides/connect.md), then run [Tell your AI about you](../prompts/00-start-here/about-me.md) once per chat so every answer is tailored to what you sell.

## Start with these 5

| # | Prompt | What you get | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [One-page vertical market snapshot](../prompts/14-industry-intelligence/vertical-snapshot.md) | A one-page view of an industry, optionally scoped to your region: size and growth, the priorities that fit what you sell with dated company proof, the top buyers, and who to call first. | 4–6 min |
| 2 | [Tier my accounts by fit and timing](../prompts/11-account-planning/propensity-tiering.md) | Up to 25 accounts scored 0–100 on fit to what you sell (hiring in your workloads, installed competitors) and timing (news, hiring, growth), tiered A, B or C with a spread you can act on, and the driver behind each score. | 4–6 min |
| 3 | [Carve balanced territories by geography](../prompts/11-account-planning/territories.md) | An industry-and-country account list, counted first, cleaned of holding and private-equity firms, and split into territories by HQ region, then balanced to within 10% on Draup-estimated IT spend, revenue or count. | 4–6 min |
| 4 | [Benchmark an account against its peers](../prompts/06-prospecting-and-lists/peer-benchmark.md) | How an account compares with its closest peers on growth, hiring in what you sell, your competitors in the stack and recent news, with discovery questions and who to call. | 2–4 min |
| 5 | [Is this inbound lead worth pursuing?](../prompts/06-prospecting-and-lists/inbound-qualification.md) | A fast Pursue now / Pursue / Nurture / Disqualify verdict on an inbound or referred account: your must-haves, urgency tied to what you sell, a bake-off check against your competitors, and a reply plan. | 1–2 min |

> **Before you trust a number**
> - Numbers: IT spend is a Draup estimate, often modelled from revenue, so use it for scale, not as a signal. Industry totals are global. Financials are in the company's reporting currency and fiscal year.
> - Freshness: news and hiring update continuously; leadership moves can lag by about three months, and brand-new executives may not appear yet.
> - People: some senior leaders have no function tag, so the prompts widen to C-level titles when needed. Draup can't search people by name.
> - Outsourcing engagements are Draup-derived records of work between a company and its providers, not published contracts.
> - Every drafted email ends with a "Check before sending" line listing any single-source or conflicting facts. Competitor tools are rated Red, Amber or Green by how entrenched they are.
> - Give the website the company actually uses (some parents use domains like home.barclays), and check names, titles and numbers before anything goes to a customer.

## Playbooks for you

- [**Account manager: risk, QBR, expansion**](../playbooks/playbook-protect-grow-customer.md): For an existing customer, see what changed, rate the renewal risk, rebuild any lost relationships, walk into the QBR with a point of view, and leave with a named expansion play.
- [**Outsourcing pursuit at a captive-centre account**](../playbooks/playbook-gcc-services-pursuit.md): Size an account's outsourcing and captive-centre (GCC) footprint, read its sourcing intent, check the main incumbent, and build a pursuit brief for the executive sponsor.
- [**Territory and list building: ICP to territories**](../playbooks/playbook-territory-list.md): Turn what your best customers have in common into a sized list, tier it on fit and timing for what you sell, carve it into balanced territories, and send the first touches to the top tier.
- [**Break into a new industry**](../playbooks/playbook-new-industry.md): Learn a new industry fast, brief the team on its language and buyers, build a first account list, and launch outbound with a point of view.
- [**Build a quarter's pipeline for one solution**](../playbooks/playbook-solution-play.md): Go from one solution area (for example cloud migration, data, ERP, security or HR tech) to a scored target list, qualified accounts with a named buyer, the incumbents to work around, and timed first touches that cite each account's own evidence.
- [**Win a deal against a named rival**](../playbooks/playbook-win-competitive-deal.md): When a named rival is in your deal, size how entrenched it is, line up the people and the arguments, arm your champion, and close on a plan the buyer has agreed.
- [**Revive a stalled deal or walk away**](../playbooks/playbook-rescue-stalled-deal.md): Decide whether a quiet deal is worth reviving, find a credible reason to re-engage, rebuild the case with the buyer's own numbers and set a realistic new close date.
- [**Take accounts from a rival software vendor**](../playbooks/playbook-competitive-takeout.md): Pick a rival, understand where it's heading, find the accounts where its product is weakest, and open those accounts with a timely, evidence-based message.
- [**Map a strategic customer, size the expansion**](../playbooks/playbook-grow-strategic-account.md): Map the whole customer, find the functions, units and sites you don't serve yet, secure the relationships that protect the account, and take a sized expansion case to your sponsor.
- [**Sales leader plan: market to coverage**](../playbooks/playbook-plan-next-quarter-territory.md): As a sales leader, size the market for what you sell, pick the region to staff, tier the accounts, carve balanced territories and set each rep's coverage, then run the plan week to week.
- [**Launch a co-sell programme with a partner**](../playbooks/playbook-partner-program-launch.md): Choose the right services partner for an industry, check it's heading the right way, build the joint pipeline, plan the first account together and arm the partner's sellers.
