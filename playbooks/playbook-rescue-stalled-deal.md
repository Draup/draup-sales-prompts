# Revive a stalled deal or walk away

> **Goal:** Decide whether a quiet deal is worth reviving, find a credible reason to re-engage, rebuild the case with the buyer's own numbers and set a realistic new close date.

**For:** Account Executive, Account & Customer Success Manager, Sales Leader & RevOps · **Time:** About 15 min with quick versions, 40–50 min with detailed · **Steps:** 5

**Step 0.** [Connect Draup for Sales](../guides/connect.md) and run [Tell your AI about you](../prompts/00-start-here/about-me.md) so your assistant knows what you sell. Then run the steps below in the same chat.

## Step 1. Re-open a stalled deal

Tests whether the reason the deal stalled still holds, lists what changed since, and drafts a re-entry email.

**Look for:** The verdict: reopen, wait or walk away. If it says wait, diarise the named event and stop here.

Open the full prompt: [Re-open a stalled deal](../prompts/08-deal-strategy/stalled-reentry.md)

Quick version:

```text
My deal at [Company] stalled on [Stalled since] because [Why it stalled]. Lead with reopen, wait or walk away, then the named person to call and the dated change since. Add the stall-reason test and a 90-word re-entry email.
```

## Step 2. New leaders and warm intro paths

Finds leaders who joined or were promoted in the buying area since the deal went quiet. Reuse the people changes from step 1; this adds where they came from.

**Look for:** A new leader with no history of the old "no", ideally one who worked at one of your customers.

Open the full prompt: [New leaders and warm intro paths](../prompts/02-buyers-and-stakeholders/new-leaders-warm-paths.md)

Quick version:

```text
New leaders at [Company] in [Workload]: bottom line and who to contact first, then hires and promotions in the last 6 months (date, name, title, came from) and any current leaders who worked at [Our customers].
```

## Step 3. Business-case inputs from their numbers

Rebuilds the value case on the buyer's own numbers, each labelled.

**Look for:** The prize as a low–high range, and which numbers are "Our assumption": those are what finance will challenge.

Open the full prompt: [Business-case inputs from their numbers](../prompts/08-deal-strategy/business-case-inputs.md)

Quick version:

```text
Business-case inputs for [What I sell] at [Company]. Lead with the prize as a range and the named person to validate it. Show their numbers, each labelled Reported, Draup estimate or Our assumption, and a worked example per lever.
```

## Step 4. Pick a realistic signature window

Maps the buyer's fiscal calendar and money-moving events so the new close date fits when budget is available.

**Look for:** The best signature window and any event in the next 6 months that would delay it.

Open the full prompt: [Pick a realistic signature window](../prompts/08-deal-strategy/budget-timing.md)

Quick version:

```text
Is [Target close date] realistic at [Company]? Lead with the best signature window and the named person to confirm it with, then fiscal quarters as calendar months, budget posture and events in the next 6 months that move money.
```

## Step 5. Reality-check a forecasted deal

Scores the revived deal on outside-in evidence before it goes back into the forecast.

**Look for:** A total of 6 or more out of 10 before calling it Best case, and the 4 questions to answer before calling it Commit.

Open the full prompt: [Reality-check a forecasted deal](../prompts/08-deal-strategy/forecast-check.md)

Quick version:

```text
Reality-check my [Forecast category] deal at [Company]: [Deal summary]. Lead with whether the category holds and the named buyer to verify with. Score Pain, Money, Timing, People and Competition 0–2 from dated evidence and give 4 questions for the rep.
```

## What you finish with

A rescue brief: why it stalled and whether that has changed, who to re-engage with a ready email, a refreshed business case, a calendar-aware close date and an honest forecast category.
