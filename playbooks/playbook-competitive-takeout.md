# Take accounts from a rival software vendor

> **Goal:** Pick a rival, understand where it's heading, find the accounts where its product is weakest, and open those accounts with a timely, evidence-based message.

**For:** Account Executive, SDR / BDR, Presales & Solutions, Sales Leader & RevOps, Marketing & ABM · **Time:** 60–90 min · **Steps:** 7

**Step 0.** [Connect Draup for Sales](../guides/connect.md) and run [Tell your AI about you](../prompts/00-start-here/about-me.md) so your assistant knows what you sell. Then run the steps below in the same chat.

## Step 1. What a rival did this quarter

Shows what the rival has done in the last 90 days: customer wins, partnerships, launches and leadership changes.

**Look for:** The Accounts to watch list. Leave fresh wins alone for now. Note any partner who is now steering deals to the rival.

Open the full prompt: [What a rival did this quarter](../prompts/09-competitive-intelligence/recent-moves.md)

Quick version:

```text
What did [Competitor] do in the last 90 days that matters to [What I sell]? Lead with the bottom line and a named person at the top account to defend, then dated moves, accounts to watch and a counter-message.
```

## Step 2. How a rival's strategy is shifting

Tells you what the rival's reps will push next quarter, and where an acquisition or cuts may distract them.

**Look for:** Themes rated Strong (call, money and people all behind them) and the trap question. Selling services? Use "Where a rival's people are going" to find delivery pressure as well.

Open the full prompt: [How a rival's strategy is shifting](../prompts/09-competitive-intelligence/strategy-shift.md)

Quick version:

```text
How has [Competitor]'s strategy shifted across its last 3 earnings calls? Lead with the bottom line and who to brief. Rate each shift Strong, Moderate or Talk only, and say what changes in my deals.
```

## Step 3. Accounts where a rival is fading

Ranks your named accounts by how weak the rival's installed products look, and names the successor tool already in place.

**Look for:** Accounts rated Fading or Thin, and any account where a third vendor is already High: there you're racing, not persuading. Selling services? Use "A rival's customers you can win" instead.

Open the full prompt: [Accounts where a rival is fading](../prompts/09-competitive-intelligence/fading-installs.md)

Quick version:

```text
For up to 8 [Accounts], check every [Competitor] product in the stack. Lead with the best takeout target and a named person there. Rate each account Entrenched, Present, Thin or Fading and name the successor already in place.
```

## Step 4. Is a competitor installed?

Confirms the rival's footprint at each of your top 3 targets and finds the stated priorities that could justify a switch.

**Look for:** A rival product with High usage in your workloads (Red) or Medium (Amber), a switch trigger with a fiscal quarter, and a likely owner. "Not detected" isn't proof they don't use it.

Open the full prompt: [Is a competitor installed?](../prompts/03-tech-stack-and-displacement/competitor-installed.md)

Quick version:

```text
Bottom line and next step first (named owner, dated why-now). Is [Competitor] installed anywhere in [Company]'s stack? Rate it Red, Amber or Green, say if [Company] owns a rival product itself, and treat it as a [Relationship].
```

## Step 5. Map the buying committee

Finds the entry point and the sponsor in the function that owns the rival's product.

**Look for:** The incumbent-tool owner (a likely blocker) and the champion candidate to open with. Go by full titles.

Open the full prompt: [Map the buying committee](../prompts/02-buyers-and-stakeholders/buying-committee.md)

Quick version:

```text
Buying committee at [Company] for [Business function or workload]: entry point and sponsor by name first, then up to 10 people with full title, unit (flag subsidiaries), likely role and linked priority. No economic buyer? Widen once to C-level.
```

## Step 6. Battlecard: beat a rival software vendor

Arms the team with the objection to expect and three ways to win against the rival.

**Look for:** The installs rated Amber or fading on the card, one trap question and one proof point you can actually cite. Mark the card internal use.

Open the full prompt: [Battlecard: beat a rival software vendor](../prompts/16-content-and-enablement/battlecard-product.md)

Quick version:

```text
Bottom line and next step first: battlecard on [Competitor] for [What I sell] — the 10 largest end users running it (no IT services firms) with usage, 180 days of moves, how we win, and the first account to try.
```

## Step 7. Signal-led emails for up to 10 accounts

Turns the freshest signal at each target into a short, relevant first email.

**Look for:** An email built on one dated signal and one stated priority, with no claims about the rival you can't back up.

Open the full prompt: [Signal-led emails for up to 10 accounts](../prompts/04-signals-and-timing/signal-to-email.md)

Quick version:

```text
Bottom line and next step first. For each of [Accounts] (up to 10), pick the 60-day signal most relevant to [What I sell] and my [Territory], fact-check it, and write a 45-word [Persona] opener ending in one ask.
```

## What you finish with

A ranked takeout list of 3–8 accounts, each with the rival's install health, the likely successor, a switch trigger, the person to contact and a first email, plus a team counter-message for the quarter.
