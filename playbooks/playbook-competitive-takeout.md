# Take accounts from a rival software vendor

> **Goal:** Pick a rival, understand where it's heading, find the accounts where its product is weakest, and open those accounts with a timely, evidence-based message.

**For:** Account Executive, SDR / BDR, Presales & Solutions, Sales Leader & RevOps, Marketing & ABM · **Time:** About 20 min with quick versions, 60–90 min with detailed · **Steps:** 7

**Step 0.** [Connect Draup for Sales](../guides/connect.md) and run [Tell your AI about you](../prompts/00-start-here/about-me.md) so your assistant knows what you sell. Then run the steps below in the same chat.

## Step 1. What a rival did this quarter

Shows what the rival has done in the last 90 days: wins, partnerships, launches and leadership changes.

**Look for:** The accounts to watch and the counter-message. Leave fresh wins alone for now.

Open the full prompt: [What a rival did this quarter](../prompts/09-competitive-intelligence/recent-moves.md)

Quick version:

```text
What did [Competitor] do in the last 90 days that matters to [What I sell]? Lead with the bottom line and a named person at the top account to defend, then dated moves, accounts to watch and a counter-message.
```

## Step 2. How a rival's strategy is shifting

Tells you what the rival's reps will push next quarter.

**Look for:** Shifts rated Strong and what they change in your deals. Selling services? Use "Where a rival's people are going" as well.

Open the full prompt: [How a rival's strategy is shifting](../prompts/09-competitive-intelligence/strategy-shift.md)

Quick version:

```text
How has [Competitor]'s strategy shifted across its last 3 earnings calls? Lead with the bottom line and who to brief. Rate each shift Strong, Moderate or Talk only, and say what changes in my deals.
```

## Step 3. Accounts where a rival is fading

Ranks your named accounts by how weak the rival's installed products look, and names the successor already in place.

**Look for:** The best takeout target, accounts where the rival is Green or fading, and any account where a third vendor is already High: there you're racing, not persuading. Selling services? Use "A rival's customers you can win" instead.

Open the full prompt: [Accounts where a rival is fading](../prompts/09-competitive-intelligence/fading-installs.md)

Quick version:

```text
For up to 8 [Accounts], check every [Competitor] product in the stack. Lead with the best takeout target and a named person there. Rate each account Red, Amber or Green (thin or fading) and name the successor already in place.
```

## Step 4. Is a competitor installed?

Adds a switch trigger and likely owner at your top 3 targets. Reuse the stack result from step 3; this adds priorities and the owner.

**Look for:** Red (High usage in your workloads), Amber (Medium or newly detected) or Green (not detected or Low), plus the owner. "Not detected" isn't proof they don't use it.

Open the full prompt: [Is a competitor installed?](../prompts/03-tech-stack-and-displacement/competitor-installed.md)

Quick version:

```text
Bottom line and next step first (named owner, dated why-now). Is [Competitor] installed at [Company] at High or Medium usage? Rate it Red/Amber/Green, flag overlaps or a product [Company] owns, then 3 bullets on how to win.
```

## Step 5. Map the buying committee

Finds the entry point and the sponsor in the function that owns the rival's product.

**Look for:** The entry point and sponsor it names, and the incumbent-tool owner, a likely blocker. Go by full titles.

Open the full prompt: [Map the buying committee](../prompts/02-buyers-and-stakeholders/buying-committee.md)

Quick version:

```text
Buying committee at [Company] for [Business function or workload]: entry point and sponsor by name first, then up to 10 people with full title, unit (flag subsidiaries), likely role and linked priority. No economic buyer? Check one more page.
```

## Step 6. Battlecard: beat a rival software vendor

Arms the team with three ways to win against the rival.

**Look for:** End users with Medium or Low usage (Amber or Green) and the "how we win" points. The trap question and objection replies are in the detailed version. Mark the card internal use.

Open the full prompt: [Battlecard: beat a rival software vendor](../prompts/16-content-and-enablement/battlecard-product.md)

Quick version:

```text
Bottom line and next step first: battlecard on [Competitor] for [What I sell]: the 10 largest parent end users in my territory running it (no IT services firms), usage, 180 days of moves, how we win, first account.
```

## Step 7. Signal-led emails for up to 10 accounts

Turns the freshest signal at each target into a short first email.

**Look for:** One fact-checked signal and one stated priority, with no claims about the rival you can't back up.

Open the full prompt: [Signal-led emails for up to 10 accounts](../prompts/04-signals-and-timing/signal-to-email.md)

Quick version:

```text
Bottom line and next step first. For each of [Accounts] (up to 10), pick the 60-day signal most relevant to [What I sell] and my [Territory], fact-check it, and write a 45-word [Persona] opener ending in one ask.
```

## What you finish with

A ranked takeout list of 3–8 accounts, each with the rival's install health, the likely successor, a switch trigger, the person to contact and a first email, plus a team counter-message for the quarter.
