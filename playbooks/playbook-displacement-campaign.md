# Win accounts from a services competitor

> **Goal:** Find accounts where a rival services firm is the incumbent, pick the ones where it's weakest, and open with peer proof and a timely, evidence-based message.

**For:** Services, SI & Staffing Seller, Account Executive, SDR / BDR, Marketing & ABM · **Time:** 45–60 min · **Steps:** 5

**Step 0.** [Connect Draup for Sales](../guides/connect.md) and run [Tell your AI about you](../prompts/00-start-here/about-me.md) so your assistant knows what you sell. Then run the steps below in the same chat.

## Step 1. Competitor's customers in my vertical

Lists the accounts where the rival delivers the workload you want to win.

**Look for:** Accounts in your territory where the rival's footprint is small. Those are usually easier to unseat than its strategic accounts.

Open the full prompt: [Competitor's customers in my vertical](../prompts/10-outsourcing-and-services/competitor-customers.md)

Quick version:

```text
Which [Vertical] companies headquartered in [Country] use [Competitor] most, per Draup? Top 20 by engagements, its share and rank at the top 8, and which accounts look contestable.
```

## Step 2. A competitor's footprint at an account

Shows, for each shortlisted account, how deep the rival is and where it's thin.

**Look for:** Wedge workloads where the rival has few engagements (3 or fewer is an editable default) and that match what you sell. Ignore catch-all workloads such as Project/Programs Management. Drop accounts where the rival is #1 across the board.

Open the full prompt: [A competitor's footprint at an account](../prompts/10-outsourcing-and-services/competitor-at-account.md)

Quick version:

```text
How big is [Competitor] at [Company] in Draup? Engagements, relationship tier, workloads and delivery locations, its rank among the account's top providers, and 3 workloads where it is thinnest.
```

## Step 3. Who's already working each priority

Shows who's already working each of the account's stated priorities.

**Look for:** A priority the incumbent doesn't cover, or covers with dated tools: that's your reason to change.

Open the full prompt: [Who's already working each priority](../prompts/03-tech-stack-and-displacement/priority-stack-incumbent-map.md)

Quick version:

```text
Map [Company]'s [Business function] priorities to the tools and service providers linked to each. Check the stack for [Competitor] products, and tag each priority Displace, Co-sell, Greenfield or Not ours for a [software / services] seller.
```

## Step 4. Peer deals for social proof

Finds recent outsourcing engagements at the account's peers in your workload, for social proof.

**Look for:** One peer move you can cite in the opener. Present it as a public industry move, not as your own customer.

Open the full prompt: [Peer deals for social proof](../prompts/10-outsourcing-and-services/peer-deals.md)

Quick version:

```text
Find the 10 most recent [Workload] outsourcing engagements at [Company]'s peers (same primary vertical and HQ country) in Draup: month, customer, provider, what the work was. Then write 3 proof lines I can use.
```

## Step 5. Signal-to-email (one or a few accounts)

Times the outreach to something that just happened and writes the opener, 5 accounts per run.

**Look for:** A signal from the last 60 days tied to the wedge priority. If there's none, park the account and revisit next month.

Open the full prompt: [Signal-to-email (one or a few accounts)](../prompts/04-signals-and-timing/signal-to-email.md)

Quick version:

```text
For each of [Accounts] (up to 5), find one deals, expansion, executive or M&A signal from the last 60 days, pair it with a stated priority, and write a short [Persona] opener ending in one easy ask. Cite sources.
```

## What you finish with

A short list of displacement targets, each with the wedge workload, the reason to change, a peer proof line and a ready-to-send opener.
