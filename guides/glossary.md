# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the terms used in the prompts and in Draup's answers.

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Account** | A company you sell to or want to sell to. |
| **Buying committee** | The group of people who decide on a purchase: the economic buyer, technical buyers, a champion, influencers and potential blockers. |
| **Business function** | A department-level area of a company. Draup uses: IT, ER&D (engineering and R&D), Finance & Accounting, Sales & Marketing, Customer Support & Service, Supply Chain, Manufacturing, Human Resources, Procurement, Legal, and Management & Strategy. |
| **Champion** | Someone inside the account who wants your solution to win and will help you navigate. |
| **Co-sell** | Selling together with a partner who already works at the account, such as a systems integrator delivering the project your product supports. |
| **Consolidation (vendor consolidation)** | When a company reduces the number of suppliers for the same work. A long tail of small providers in one workload often signals a consolidation opportunity. |
| **Displacement** | Replacing an incumbent supplier or product at an account. |
| **Draup opportunities** | Entry points Draup suggests for an account, built from its stated priorities. If you save your solution offering in your Draup profile, they also include executive moves and research-intent signals. |
| **Economic buyer** | The person who controls the budget and signs off. In banking and consulting this is usually an MD, EVP, "Head of" or C-level, not a VP. |
| **Engagement** | A recorded piece of work between a customer and a service provider, such as an application-support contract. Draup counts active engagements to show how deep a provider is at an account. Engagement records are Draup-derived, not published contracts. |
| **Fiscal year / quarter** | A company's own accounting year. Draup shows the company's fiscal labels, which can differ from the calendar; a fiscal "2027 Q3" can fall in calendar 2026. |
| **GCC (global capability centre)** | A company's own offshore or nearshore centre for technology, engineering, finance or operations work, for example a bank's technology centre in Bengaluru. Draup tags these locations. |
| **BOT (build-operate-transfer)** | A model where a services firm builds and runs a centre for a client, then hands it over to the client to own. |
| **Incumbent** | The supplier or product already in place for a given area of work. |
| **IT spend** | Draup's estimate of what a company spends on technology, split into on-premise software, internal IT staff, IT outsourcing and hardware. For many companies it is modelled from revenue, so treat it as an estimate. |
| **MEDDPICC** | A deal-qualification framework: Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Paper process, Identify pain, Champion, Competition. |
| **Opportunity Index** | A Draup score summarising how attractive an account is, based on its size, spend and activity. Use it to sort lists, not as a forecast. |
| **Outsourced headcount** | Draup's estimate of how many people service providers have working for a company. It is modelled, not reported. |
| **Primary vertical / industry** | The main industry Draup assigns to a company. Lists built on the primary industry are cleaner than lists built on any industry tag, which also pulls in companies with a secondary tag. |
| **Priority** | A strategic initiative a company has stated publicly, in an earnings call or the news. Draup tags each one with business function, workloads and quarter. |
| **Relationship tier** | How strategic a service provider is at an account: strategic partner, operational, specialist or limited. |
| **Re-insourcing** | When a company brings outsourced work back in-house, often visible as rising in-house hiring for work it used to outsource. |
| **Signal** | A dated news event that could create a reason to reach out: a leadership change, funding, M&A, layoffs, an expansion, a partnership or a product launch. |
| **Talent flow** | Where a company's people come from and go to: which companies it hires from and loses people to, and in which cities. |
| **Usage strength** | How strongly Draup's evidence shows a product is in use at an account (High, Medium or Low), together with the date it was last seen. |
| **Why now** | The specific, recent reason a buyer should act this quarter. |
| **Workload** | A type of work, finer-grained than a function, such as Cloud Computing, Data Engineering, Application Development & Maintenance, or AI & Data Science. Draup uses workloads to connect priorities, hiring, technology and outsourcing. |
| **Whitespace** | Areas at an account where you don't sell yet but could. |
