# Playbooks

Multi-step workflows. Run the steps in order in one conversation; each step names the prompt id to use.

## Be ready for a first meeting

**Goal:** Walk into a first meeting knowing the account, the people in the room and the questions that will earn a second meeting.
**For:** Account Executive, SDR / BDR, Presales & Solutions, Services, SI & Staffing Seller · **Time:** About 12 min with quick versions, 25–35 min with detailed

1. **10-minute pre-call snapshot** (`research-precall-snapshot`): Gives you the 10-minute picture: size, two recent events, hiring in your function and its closest priority. *Look for:* One priority and one dated event you can mention in the first two minutes. Check the company name it used is the parent, not a subsidiary.
2. **Map the buying committee** (`people-buying-committee`): Shows who owns the problem and the budget in the function you sell into. Run it in the same chat so it reuses the company from step 1. *Look for:* The entry point and sponsor it names first, and anyone flagged as subsidiary staff. If no C-level or "Head of" name appears, plan to ask who signs.
3. **Discovery questions built on their data** (`people-discovery-questions`): Turns the facts into hypothesis-led questions. Reuse the result from steps 1 and 2: run it in the same chat so it doesn't pull them again. *Look for:* The 3 questions you'll definitely ask, and the one that tests whether the priority has budget this year.
4. **Battlecard: beat a rival software vendor** (`content-battlecard-product`): Optional: run it when a known rival is in the deal. Selling services? Use "Battlecard: beat a rival services firm" instead. *Look for:* The "how we win" points and any of the 10 end users that are this account's peers. For the objection to expect and trap questions, use the detailed version.

**Finish with:** A one-page meeting brief: account snapshot, who's who, an opening line, your discovery questions with the evidence behind each, and (if a rival is in play) the objection to expect.

## Take accounts from a rival services firm

**Goal:** Find accounts where a rival services firm is the incumbent, pick the ones where it's weakest, and open with peer proof and a timely, evidence-based message.
**For:** Services, SI & Staffing Seller, Account Executive, SDR / BDR, Marketing & ABM · **Time:** About 15 min with quick versions, 45–60 min with detailed

1. **A rival's customers you can win** (`services-competitor-customers`): Lists the accounts where the rival delivers the work you want to win, with its share and rank at the top 8. *Look for:* The 3 most contestable accounts and the rival's share at each. Under 10% of the account's active engagements (Green) is usually easiest to unseat; 30% or more (Red) is entrenched.
2. **A rival's footprint and your wedge** (`services-competitor-at-account`): For each of the 3 contestable accounts from step 1, shows how deep the rival is and where it's thin. Reuse step 1's share figures; this adds tier, delivery locations and thin workloads. *Look for:* The 3 thin workloads that match what you sell (ignore catch-alls such as Project/Programs Management). Drop accounts where the rival is #1 with a Strategic tier across the board.
3. **Who's already working each priority** (`tech-priority-stack-incumbent-map`): Shows who's already working each of the account's stated priorities. *Look for:* A priority tagged Displace or Greenfield in a thin workload from step 2: that's your reason to change.
4. **Peers' outsourcing deals for social proof** (`services-peer-deals`): Finds recent engagements at the account's peers in your workload, for social proof. *Look for:* One of the 3 proof lines you can cite in the opener, as a public industry move, never as your own customer.
5. **Signal-led emails for up to 10 accounts** (`signals-signal-to-email`): Times the outreach to something that just happened and writes the opener for each shortlisted account. *Look for:* A fact-checked signal from the last 60 days tied to the wedge priority, sent to the person named in step 2 or 3. No signal? Park the account and revisit next month.

**Finish with:** A short list of displacement targets, each with the wedge workload, the reason to change, a peer proof line and a ready-to-send opener.

## Monday routine: who to contact this week

**Goal:** Spend about half an hour each Monday finding which accounts and industry moves deserve action this week, and send the first touches.
**For:** Account Executive, Account & Customer Success Manager, SDR / BDR · **Time:** About 15 min each week with quick versions, 30–40 min with detailed

1. **Weekly trigger sweep for my book** (`signals-weekly-book-sweep`): Scans up to 10 named accounts for real triggers in the window and drops the noise. *Look for:* Accounts with a leadership change, new site, deal, acquisition, funding or layoff dated inside the window, plus the nearest item outside it for quiet accounts. Outages and breaches count only if they bear on what you sell.
2. **Weekly industry signal digest** (`signals-vertical-digest`): Shows what moved across your industry, including companies not yet on your list. *Look for:* Peer moves you can use as social proof, and the 3–5 accounts it says to touch; add any new name to your book.
3. **What changed since [Date]** (`signals-change-log`): For the 2–3 hottest accounts from step 1, lists what changed since your last touch. Reuse the result from step 1 for news; this adds people, tools and hiring. *Look for:* The verdict (customer: defend, expand or monitor; lost deal: reopen, wait or watch) and the named person behind it.
4. **Signal-led emails for up to 10 accounts** (`signals-signal-to-email`): Turns the week's best triggers into sourced openers. Reuse the triggers from steps 1–3: run it in the same chat so it doesn't search again. *Look for:* One fact-checked signal per account in a 45-word opener; send today to the person named in step 1 or 3, and hold any account whose signal failed the fact check.

**Finish with:** 3–5 accounts to contact this week, each with the trigger, its date, the person, the angle and a ready-to-send opener.

## Account manager: risk, QBR, expansion

**Goal:** For an existing customer, see what changed, rate the renewal risk, rebuild any lost relationships, walk into the QBR with a point of view, and leave with a named expansion play.
**For:** Account & Customer Success Manager, Account Executive, Sales Leader & RevOps · **Time:** About 15 min with quick versions, 45–60 min with detailed

1. **What changed since [Date]** (`signals-change-log`): Lists what changed at the customer since a date you choose (up to 12 months back). *Look for:* New leaders, new tools, hiring shifts or restructuring in the areas you serve, and the verdict: defend, expand or monitor.
2. **Renewal risk check (red, amber, green)** (`plan-risk-check`): Rates renewal and relationship risk Red, Amber or Green with dated evidence, including your sponsor. Reuse step 1's changes as context; this adds sponsor, exits, layoffs and competitor usage. *Look for:* Any Red row, and whether your sponsor is still in role. A rival product with High usage in your workloads is Red, Medium or newly detected is Amber; Green means nothing found (unverified).
3. **New leaders and warm intro paths** (`people-new-leaders-warm-paths`): Run it only if steps 1–2 show a new leader or a sponsor change; reuse those names and add where they came from and the warm paths to them. *Look for:* Decision-makers you haven't met, especially any who worked at one of your customers. Book the introduction before the renewal conversation starts.
4. **QBR prep: talking points and asks** (`plan-qbr-prep`): Sets the outcomes you delivered against what changed in your sponsor's function. Reuse the result from step 1: tell it to use those changes instead of pulling them again, and paste your outcomes. *Look for:* 3 talking points that link a dated change to value you delivered, and 3 asks: expansion, reference, executive sponsor.
5. **Best entry points: expand or new** (`plan-entry-points`): Turns the account into the five best expansion moves for what you sell, each marked Expand or New. *Look for:* The entry point with the strongest dated evidence and the fewest incumbents. Make it the expansion ask in the QBR, with its likely owner as your next meeting.

**Finish with:** A dated change log, a RAG risk view with owners, a plan to meet new leaders, a QBR brief with talking points and 3 asks, and one expansion play with a named owner.

## Outsourcing pursuit at a captive-centre account

**Goal:** Size an account's outsourcing and captive-centre (GCC) footprint, read its sourcing intent, check the main incumbent, and build a pursuit brief for the executive sponsor.
**For:** Services, SI & Staffing Seller, Sales Leader & RevOps, Presales & Solutions · **Time:** About 15 min with quick versions, 55–70 min with detailed

1. **Outsourcing footprint and consolidation wedge** (`services-outsourcing-footprint`): Shows who the account outsources to, for which work, and how concentrated it is. *Look for:* Workloads with many small providers (a consolidation play) or one dominant incumbent (a displacement or second-vendor play). Note the #1 IT provider: step 4 uses it.
2. **Their capability centres and the services play** (`services-gcc-opportunity`): Maps the account's company-owned offshore centres: where, which functions are hiring and roughly how big. Name 3 peers in its industry when you run it, or it will guess. *Look for:* Whether the centres look expanding, flat or shrinking, and which functions sit there: optimisation, centre-of-excellence expansion or a build-operate-transfer play. Treat site size as indicative.
3. **Is outsourced work coming back in-house?** (`services-reinsourcing-risk`): Checks whether the account is building in-house what it used to outsource. Run it on the function closest to what you sell, and reuse the result from step 1 (outsourced workloads) and step 2 (capability centres) instead of pulling them again. *Look for:* Workloads labelled re-insourcing risk: position augmentation or transition support there, not pure outsourcing. Capability gap and cost takeout are your openings.
4. **A rival's footprint and your wedge** (`services-competitor-at-account`): Shows how deep the incumbent is and where it's thin. For [Competitor], use the #1 IT provider from step 1. *Look for:* The 3 thin workloads you'll lead with, and the incumbent's strongest areas to avoid.
5. **Brief for a C-level pursuit meeting** (`plan-cxo-pursuit-brief`): Pulls it together into a Go or Not-yet brief. For [Business function], use the function closest to what you sell, and tell it to use the wedges from steps 1 and 4 and the labels from step 3. *Look for:* The 3 workloads to lead with, the named buyer, the Go or Not-yet call and the 90-day plan.

**Finish with:** A CXO pursuit brief: outsourcing and GCC picture, sourcing intent by workload, the incumbent's weak spots, the lead offer, buyers, a go/no-go call and a 90-day plan.

## Co-sell an account with the right partner

**Goal:** Find where partners are already embedded at a target account, pick the right one to co-sell with, and open jointly.
**For:** Partnerships & Alliances, Account Executive, Services, SI & Staffing Seller · **Time:** About 12 min with quick versions, 35–45 min with detailed

1. **Which providers to partner with or replace** (`services-partner-map`): Shows which providers and vendors are linked to each priority at the account, with engagements counted in your function. *Look for:* Providers tagged Partner on a priority next to yours: they already have access and credibility.
2. **Pick the SI to co-sell with** (`services-cosell-partner`): Ranks the providers that deliver your workload at the account and know your technology. Reuse step 1's provider list; this adds workload depth and technology experience. *Look for:* The provider it says to call first: deepest in your workload (engagements in that work, not account-wide) with experience in your technology. Even better if step 1 tagged it Partner.
3. **Profile a services firm, rival or partner** (`services-competitor-profile`): Profiles the partner you picked: top customers, workloads and delivery hubs. *Look for:* Overlap with your target list and industry, and delivery hubs near the account's sites: the evidence for your co-sell ask.
4. **ABM openers for 10 accounts at once** (`content-abm-messages`): Writes first-touch openers for the shared accounts, up to 10 per pass. *Look for:* Strong rows (signal ties to the priority) first. For a joint note both teams can send, use the detailed version with the co-sell partner input.

**Finish with:** A co-sell pick with evidence (where the partner is embedded and how it overlaps with your list) and joint openers for the shared target accounts.

## Territory and list building: ICP to territories

**Goal:** Turn what your best customers have in common into a sized list, tier it on fit and timing for what you sell, carve it into balanced territories, and send the first touches to the top tier.
**For:** SDR / BDR, Account Executive, Marketing & ABM, Sales Leader & RevOps · **Time:** About 15 min with quick versions, 45–60 min with detailed

1. **Rewrite our ICP from best customers** (`leaders-icp-refinement`): Finds the traits your best customers share, including activity in the work you sell, and turns them into a look-alike definition. *Look for:* The definition and how many new companies it returns (15–100 is workable). The customer data check (subsidiaries, venture arms) is in the detailed version.
2. **Size your ideal-customer list** (`prospect-icp-list`): Builds and counts the list. Reuse the definition from step 1 as its inputs, so the counts match. *Look for:* A count that applies every filter, with distributors set aside, sized for your team, plus the 5 to start with.
3. **Tier my accounts by fit and timing** (`plan-propensity-tiering`): Scores up to 25 accounts from step 2 on fit and timing for what you sell. *Look for:* A real spread across A, B and C; any account capped at C for low fit; and any website matched by name, with its score, to check.
4. **Carve balanced territories by geography** (`plan-territories`): Carves the market into balanced territories, keeping whole regions together. Reuse the industry, country and size cut from step 2 so the counts match. *Look for:* No territory more than about 10% off balance, holding and private-equity firms removed, and every single-account move listed. Over 100 accounts? Raise the size threshold first.
5. **ABM openers for 10 accounts at once** (`content-abm-messages`): Gives the A-tier accounts from step 3 a personalised first touch, up to 10 per pass. *Look for:* Strong rows (signal ties to the priority) go to SDRs this week; the rest go to a nurture track.

**Finish with:** A refined ICP, a sized list, A/B/C tiers with the scoring shown, territories per rep, and ready-to-send openers for the top tier.

## Break into a new industry

**Goal:** Learn a new industry fast, brief the team on its language and buyers, build a first account list, and launch outbound with a point of view.
**For:** Marketing & ABM, Sales Leader & RevOps, SDR / BDR, Account Executive · **Time:** About 15 min with quick versions, 45–60 min with detailed

1. **One-page vertical market snapshot** (`market-vertical-snapshot`): Gives a one-page view of the industry: size and growth, priorities with dated company proof and the top buyers. *Look for:* The 2–3 priorities closest to what you sell. Industry totals are global; if the industry returned zeros, check which parent industry it used.
2. **Brief reps on a new industry** (`leaders-vertical-enablement`): Turns the industry into a rep brief: the words to use, who buys at one reference account and where your rivals sit. Reuse the priorities from step 1 if they're in this chat. *Look for:* The buyer titles at the reference account and the 10 terms your reps must use. If it says Draup has no industry priorities, the funding table is company proof, not industry-wide.
3. **Size your ideal-customer list** (`prospect-icp-list`): Builds and sizes the target list in the industry. *Look for:* A count that applies every filter, with distributors set aside, and the 5 accounts to start with.
4. **Build an industry point of view** (`market-industry-pov`): Turns the industry's priorities into a point of view with dated company proof. Reuse the priorities from steps 1–2 instead of pulling them again. *Look for:* The 35-word POV you'd say to any executive in the industry, backed by a dated company example.
5. **3-email sequence for one industry persona** (`content-email-sequence`): Writes a 3-email sequence for your main persona on those priorities, and names the first account to load. *Look for:* Emails that open with a real, dated industry move, never presented as your customer, and the first account to load.

**Finish with:** An industry brief, a rep enablement sheet, a first target list, a one-line point of view and a ready-to-load email sequence.

## Build a quarter's pipeline for one solution

**Goal:** Go from one solution area (for example cloud migration, data, ERP, security or HR tech) to a scored target list, qualified accounts with a named buyer, the incumbents to work around, and timed first touches that cite each account's own evidence.
**For:** Account Executive, SDR / BDR, Sales Leader & RevOps, Services, SI & Staffing Seller, Marketing & ABM · **Time:** About 20 min with quick versions, 60–75 min with detailed

1. **Find accounts ripe for cloud migration** (`plays-cloud-targets`): Builds the evidence-scored target list for your solution area in one industry and country. The example is cloud migration; swap in the matching target-list prompt for data, ERP, security, HR tech and the others. *Look for:* The top 3, the evidence behind each score and who to call. The set-aside list (misfiled, HQ elsewhere, companies that sell the technology themselves) is in the detailed version.
2. **Is this account ready for cloud migration?** (`plays-cloud-readiness`): Qualifies each top account from step 1. Use the readiness prompt that matches your solution. *Look for:* A strong score with a dated why-now and a named owner; if the score is low, move to the next account rather than forcing it.
3. **Map the buying committee** (`people-buying-committee`): Turns the owner from step 2 into a full committee in the matching function. Reuse the owner named in step 2 as the starting point. *Look for:* The entry point and sponsor it names first, by full title, and anyone flagged as subsidiary staff.
4. **Who's already working each priority** (`tech-priority-stack-incumbent-map`): Shows, for each priority in the function, which tools and providers already work it. *Look for:* One priority tagged Displace or Greenfield: that's your wedge.
5. **Peers' outsourcing deals for social proof** (`services-peer-deals`): Finds recent engagements at each account's peers in your workload, so the first touch carries social proof. *Look for:* One of the 3 proof lines per account, cited as a public industry example, never as your own customer.
6. **Signal-led emails for up to 10 accounts** (`signals-signal-to-email`): Writes the first touch from the freshest relevant signal. Reuse the why-now from step 2 if it's under 60 days old. *Look for:* One fact-checked signal in a 45-word opener ending in one ask, sent to the entry point from step 3. No signal in 60 days? Keep the account in nurture.

**Finish with:** A quarter's pipeline for one solution area: a scored target list, a readiness verdict and wedge for the top accounts, a named champion and sponsor for each, a peer proof line and a ready-to-send opener.

## A week of trigger-based outreach

**Goal:** Turn one week of real account triggers into timely, multi-channel outreach: every message tied to a dated public fact, routed to the right person, with a call and voicemail behind each email and a clean re-open for stalled deals.
**For:** SDR / BDR, Account Executive, Account & Customer Success Manager · **Time:** About 20 min of quick runs across the week, 60–90 min with detailed

1. **Weekly trigger sweep for my book** (`signals-weekly-book-sweep`): Monday: finds the week's real triggers across up to 10 accounts. *Look for:* Accounts with a dated trigger in the window and its type: leadership change, funding, acquisition, restructuring, new site or partnership. Accounts with nothing new feed Friday's step.
2. **Email a newly appointed executive** (`outreach-new-executive`): Tuesday: writes the trigger email. Use this prompt for leadership changes; swap in the matching trigger prompt for funding, acquisitions, restructuring, new sites, hiring surges or new technology. *Look for:* One confirmed public fact and one stated priority per email, and nothing that reveals how you found it. For restructuring triggers, use the detailed version, which rates sensitivity.
3. **Emails to three buyers at one account** (`outreach-multithread`): Wednesday: for the one or two accounts that matter most, opens three threads (economic buyer, technical buyer, champion), each on a different priority. *Look for:* A named person for each role, or the title to find. Stagger the sends; never reuse a paragraph across threads.
4. **Cold-call opener, talk track, objections** (`outreach-cold-call`): Thursday: call the people you emailed. Reuse the facts from steps 2 and 3 so the call matches the email. *Look for:* An opener that continues the email they received. Keep installed-tool findings in your prep notes, not in the script.
5. **Voicemail script plus follow-up email** (`outreach-voicemail`): Thursday, when they don't pick up: a voicemail and a same-day follow-up email. Reuse the same fact as the step 4 call. *Look for:* The same fact in the voicemail and the email, and a yes-or-no reply option in the email.
6. **Re-engage a stalled or ghosted account** (`outreach-reengage`): Friday: for stalled or ghosted accounts, including those with nothing new in step 1, lists what changed and writes a re-engagement and a break-up email. *Look for:* The verdict: reopen now, reopen via a new person, or wait. If a new leader appeared, send the new-person version too.

**Finish with:** A logged week of outreach: each Act now account touched by email, call and voicemail on a dated fact, the top accounts multi-threaded to three people, and every stalled deal either re-opened, handed to a new contact, or closed out politely with a date to revisit.

## Win a deal against a named rival

**Goal:** When a named rival is in your deal, size how entrenched it is, line up the people and the arguments, arm your champion, and close on a plan the buyer has agreed.
**For:** Account Executive, Presales & Solutions, Services, SI & Staffing Seller, Sales Leader & RevOps · **Time:** About 15 min with quick versions, 45–60 min with detailed

1. **Win plan against a rival** (`deals-competitive-strategy`): Shows the rival's footprint at this account, its likely pitch and your wedge. *Look for:* The Red, Amber or Green rating (Green = not detected or Low usage; for a services rival, under 10% of active engagements) and the 3 landmine questions. Red across the whole workload? Narrow scope to your wedge.
2. **Map the buying committee** (`people-buying-committee`): Maps who buys, influences or could block in the deal's function. *Look for:* Anyone leaning to the rival, an economic buyer you haven't met, and subsidiary staff to discount. Go by full titles, not seniority labels.
3. **Answer objections with their own facts** (`deals-objection-handler`): Prepares evidence-backed answers to the pushback a rival's champion will feed the buyer. *Look for:* Objections it marks real, not cover: change scope or timing there rather than arguing.
4. **Arm your champion to sell internally** (`deals-champion-pack`): Gives your champion a forwardable case in their own company's language. *Look for:* The 3 dated why-now facts and the 150-word internal email your champion is comfortable sending.
5. **Mutual action plan on their calendar** (`deals-mutual-action-plan`): Locks the path to signature into the buyer's own calendar before the rival can slow it down. *Look for:* Whether the close date comes back Achievable, Tight or Unrealistic, and calendar events that could shift dates.

**Finish with:** A one-page win plan: the rival's footprint and likely pitch, your wedge and landmines, the people map, objection answers, a champion pack and a mutual action plan with dates.

## Revive a stalled deal or walk away

**Goal:** Decide whether a quiet deal is worth reviving, find a credible reason to re-engage, rebuild the case with the buyer's own numbers and set a realistic new close date.
**For:** Account Executive, Account & Customer Success Manager, Sales Leader & RevOps · **Time:** About 15 min with quick versions, 40–50 min with detailed

1. **Re-open a stalled deal** (`deals-stalled-reentry`): Tests whether the reason the deal stalled still holds, lists what changed since, and drafts a re-entry email. *Look for:* The verdict: reopen, wait or walk away. If it says wait, diarise the named event and stop here.
2. **New leaders and warm intro paths** (`people-new-leaders-warm-paths`): Finds leaders who joined or were promoted in the buying area since the deal went quiet. Reuse the people changes from step 1; this adds where they came from. *Look for:* A new leader with no history of the old "no", ideally one who worked at one of your customers.
3. **Business-case inputs from their numbers** (`deals-business-case-inputs`): Rebuilds the value case on the buyer's own numbers, each labelled. *Look for:* The prize as a low–high range, and which numbers are "Our assumption": those are what finance will challenge.
4. **Pick a realistic signature window** (`deals-budget-timing`): Maps the buyer's fiscal calendar and money-moving events so the new close date fits when budget is available. *Look for:* The best signature window and any event in the next 6 months that would delay it.
5. **Reality-check a forecasted deal** (`deals-forecast-check`): Scores the revived deal on outside-in evidence before it goes back into the forecast. *Look for:* A total of 6 or more out of 10 before calling it Best case, and the 4 questions to answer before calling it Commit.

**Finish with:** A rescue brief: why it stalled and whether that has changed, who to re-engage with a ready email, a refreshed business case, a calendar-aware close date and an honest forecast category.

## Take accounts from a rival software vendor

**Goal:** Pick a rival, understand where it's heading, find the accounts where its product is weakest, and open those accounts with a timely, evidence-based message.
**For:** Account Executive, SDR / BDR, Presales & Solutions, Sales Leader & RevOps, Marketing & ABM · **Time:** About 20 min with quick versions, 60–90 min with detailed

1. **What a rival did this quarter** (`compete-recent-moves`): Shows what the rival has done in the last 90 days: wins, partnerships, launches and leadership changes. *Look for:* The accounts to watch and the counter-message. Leave fresh wins alone for now.
2. **How a rival's strategy is shifting** (`compete-strategy-shift`): Tells you what the rival's reps will push next quarter. *Look for:* Shifts rated Strong and what they change in your deals. Selling services? Use "Where a rival's people are going" as well.
3. **Accounts where a rival is fading** (`compete-fading-installs`): Ranks your named accounts by how weak the rival's installed products look, and names the successor already in place. *Look for:* The best takeout target, accounts where the rival is Green or fading, and any account where a third vendor is already High: there you're racing, not persuading. Selling services? Use "A rival's customers you can win" instead.
4. **Is a competitor installed?** (`tech-competitor-installed`): Adds a switch trigger and likely owner at your top 3 targets. Reuse the stack result from step 3; this adds priorities and the owner. *Look for:* Red (High usage in your workloads), Amber (Medium or newly detected) or Green (not detected or Low), plus the owner. "Not detected" isn't proof they don't use it.
5. **Map the buying committee** (`people-buying-committee`): Finds the entry point and the sponsor in the function that owns the rival's product. *Look for:* The entry point and sponsor it names, and the incumbent-tool owner, a likely blocker. Go by full titles.
6. **Battlecard: beat a rival software vendor** (`content-battlecard-product`): Arms the team with three ways to win against the rival. *Look for:* End users with Medium or Low usage (Amber or Green) and the "how we win" points. The trap question and objection replies are in the detailed version. Mark the card internal use.
7. **Signal-led emails for up to 10 accounts** (`signals-signal-to-email`): Turns the freshest signal at each target into a short first email. *Look for:* One fact-checked signal and one stated priority, with no claims about the rival you can't back up.

**Finish with:** A ranked takeout list of 3–8 accounts, each with the rival's install health, the likely successor, a switch trigger, the person to contact and a first email, plus a team counter-message for the quarter.

## Map a strategic customer, size the expansion

**Goal:** Map the whole customer, find the functions, units and sites you don't serve yet, secure the relationships that protect the account, and take a sized expansion case to your sponsor.
**For:** Account & Customer Success Manager, Account Executive, Sales Leader & RevOps, Services, SI & Staffing Seller · **Time:** About 25 min with quick versions, 75–100 min with detailed

1. **Map a customer's units, sites and leaders** (`expand-account-map`): Gives you the shape of the account: units, regions, top sites and recent acquisitions. *Look for:* The 3 biggest open areas and the Ours / Adjacent / Open labels. Tell it where you are today or the map won't show white space.
2. **Find uncovered functions worth selling to** (`expand-function-whitespace`): Scores every function on priorities, hiring and outsourced work against what you already sell. *Look for:* The top uncovered function and the named leader to approach, with a dated why-now.
3. **New budget holders outside our footprint** (`expand-new-budget-holders`): Finds senior leaders new in role in the functions you don't cover. *Look for:* The leader to contact first, and any announced changes that could freeze or reopen decisions.
4. **Land at one site, expand to others** (`expand-site-to-site`): Optional: run it when you've landed at one site or capability centre. Reuse the sites from step 1 instead of pulling them again. *Look for:* The next site to approach and its named local leader your sponsor can introduce.
5. **Check sponsor risk and relationship gaps** (`expand-sponsor-coverage`): Checks how exposed you are to one sponsor. Reuse the leaders from steps 2–3 as the people to cover. *Look for:* The sponsor's risk rating, empty economic-buyer cells and the next relationship to build. Keep this internal.
6. **Expansion business case from growth units** (`expand-business-case`): Builds a sized case that rides the customer's own growth story. *Look for:* Priorities in management's words, a spend range with the formula shown, and the 150-word summary your sponsor can forward.
7. **Draft an account plan** (`plan-account-plan`): Pulls everything into an account plan. Reuse the results from steps 1–6: run it in the same chat and tell it not to re-pull the committee or priorities. *Look for:* The first play and named contact, and ranked plays that match the white space and new budget holders you found.

**Finish with:** An account growth plan: a one-page map with white space marked, the top expansion function and site, the new budget holders and relationship gaps to close, and a sized business case ready for your sponsor.

## Sales leader plan: market to coverage

**Goal:** As a sales leader, size the market for what you sell, pick where to add the next rep, tier the accounts, carve balanced territories, set coverage and next quarter's targets, then run the plan week to week.
**For:** Sales Leader & RevOps, Marketing & ABM · **Time:** About 25 min with quick versions, 70–90 min with detailed

1. **One-page vertical market snapshot** (`market-vertical-snapshot`): Gives the industry picture: size and growth, top priorities and the top buyers. *Look for:* The 2–3 priorities closest to what you sell. Industry totals are global, so don't use them to compare accounts.
2. **Which region gets the next rep** (`leaders-territory-potential`): Compares 2–5 countries for one industry, or 2–4 industries in one country, on accounts active in your workloads, rivals in use and revenue. *Look for:* The segment with the most workload-active accounts per rep: add your current coverage so the per-rep score can change the answer. Big groups headquartered elsewhere are listed in the detailed version.
3. **Tier my accounts by fit and timing** (`plan-propensity-tiering`): Tiers up to 25 of the winning segment's top accounts on fit and timing. *Look for:* A real spread across A, B and C, accounts capped at C for low fit, and the driver behind each A account.
4. **Carve balanced territories by geography** (`plan-territories`): Carves the winning segment into balanced territories by geography. Reuse step 2's country, industry and size cut so the counts match. *Look for:* No territory more than about 10% off balance, holding or private-equity firms excluded, and every single-account move listed.
5. **Sort my book into coverage tiers** (`leaders-book-segmentation`): Sets coverage inside each territory: Strategic, Growth, Maintain or Deprioritise. Reuse step 3's account list rather than looking the accounts up again. *Look for:* No rep with more than about 5 Strategic accounts, and every flagged account (spin-off, merger, private) checked by hand.
6. **Next quarter's target accounts, ranked by triggers** (`leaders-pipeline-targets`): Sets next quarter's targets: searches 90-day news account by account for the top 20 workload-active accounts and ranks them by triggers. *Look for:* Accounts tagged Act this month (ideally two trigger types) and the counts per tag for SDR targets. Accounts beyond the top 20 are "not searched", not quiet.
7. **Brief reps on a new industry** (`leaders-vertical-enablement`): Optional: run it when a territory includes an industry the team is new to. *Look for:* The buyer titles and 10 terms for the enablement session. If it says Draup has no industry priorities, the funding table is company proof.
8. **Which accounts each rep works this week** (`leaders-coverage-check`): Runs the plan week to week: checks each rep's accounts for fresh triggers and names this week's 5 actions. *Look for:* Owners with several Strong triggers (they need help) and owners whose whole book is quiet (they need new accounts).

**Finish with:** A sized market view, where to add the next rep, tiered accounts, balanced territories, a coverage model, next quarter's target list and a weekly action list. Ask any leader prompt for a slide version (5 bullets plus 1 table) for the CRO update.

## Launch a co-sell programme with a partner

**Goal:** Choose the right services partner for an industry, check it's heading the right way, build the joint pipeline, plan the first account together and arm the partner's sellers.
**For:** Partnerships & Alliances, Sales Leader & RevOps, Marketing & ABM · **Time:** About 20 min with quick versions, 55–70 min with detailed

1. **Which SIs to recruit in an industry** (`partners-recruit-si-shortlist`): Finds the SIs that already deliver your workload in the industry and work with your technology. *Look for:* Firms marked Recruit first (high in both lists and 3+ technology-related engagements), your current partners first. Single-account depth is flagged in the detailed version.
2. **Compare candidate partners head to head** (`partners-compare-candidates`): Puts the 2–3 best candidates from step 1 side by side with weights you control. Reuse step 1's engagement counts if they're in this chat. *Look for:* Whether the pick survives the sensitivity check, and each candidate's main risk (concentration, offshore-heavy, thin technology work).
3. **Is this partner rising or slowing?** (`partners-momentum-check`): Checks the chosen partner is rising, not slowing. *Look for:* A Rising or Steady verdict, and any signal scored 0: those are the questions for your first partner meeting.
4. **Accounts a partner can open for you** (`partners-sourced-pipeline`): Lists the accounts in your segment where the partner already delivers work and your product isn't in the stack yet. *Look for:* The band of matching accounts and, among the top 5, those with Deep partner depth in a workload next to yours: the ones the partner can credibly open.
5. **Joint account plan with a partner** (`partners-joint-account-plan`): Turns the best account from step 4 into a one-page joint plan. *Look for:* Joint workloads with priorities linked to the partner, and a named owner on each side for days 1–30.
6. **Enablement brief for a partner's sellers** (`partners-enablement-brief`): Gives the partner's sellers a short brief in their own terms. Reuse step 4's account list as a cross-check for warm starts. *Look for:* The first 3 customers it names, which run your platform with or without the rival: lead the enablement session with them.

**Finish with:** A chosen partner with evidence behind the choice, a sized joint pipeline, a signed-off plan for the first account and an enablement brief the partner's sellers can use on their next call.

## Run an ABM campaign for 10–20 accounts

**Goal:** Pick 10–20 target accounts, tier them, write each one a personalised opener from its own priorities and signals, multithread the top accounts, nurture the rest and refresh the list every week.
**For:** Marketing & ABM, SDR / BDR, Account Executive · **Time:** About 20 min with quick versions, 50–70 min with detailed

1. **Tier my accounts by fit and timing** (`plan-propensity-tiering`): Scores up to 25 candidate accounts on fit and timing for what you sell; keep the top 10–20 for the campaign. *Look for:* A and B accounts for the campaign, any account capped at C for low fit, and websites matched by name, with their score, to check before you load them.
2. **ABM openers for 10 accounts at once** (`content-abm-messages`): Writes a 2-sentence opener per account from one priority and one 60-day signal. Run it twice for 20 accounts: accounts 1–10, then 11–20. *Look for:* Strong rows (signal ties to the priority) launch in week 1; the rest go to the nurture sequence in step 4. Check no opener pitches a technology account its own product category.
3. **Emails to three buyers at one account** (`outreach-multithread`): For the 2–3 A-tier accounts with Strong rows, opens three threads (economic buyer, technical buyer, champion). Reuse step 2's priority and signal for that account. *Look for:* A named person for each role, a different priority per thread and a staggered send plan.
4. **3-email sequence for one industry persona** (`content-email-sequence`): Writes the 3-email nurture sequence for your main persona in the campaign's main industry. *Look for:* Emails that open with a dated industry move, never presented as your customer. Load the accounts without a Strong row from step 2.
5. **Weekly trigger sweep for my book** (`signals-weekly-book-sweep`): Every Monday of the campaign, checks up to 10 campaign accounts for new triggers (run it twice for 20). *Look for:* A new trigger at a nurture account: move it to a personal touch that week, using the person and so-what it names.

**Finish with:** A tiered campaign list of 10–20 accounts, a sourced opener for each, three-person threads at the top accounts, a nurture sequence for the rest and a weekly trigger check that moves accounts from nurture to personal outreach.
